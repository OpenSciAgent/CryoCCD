CryoCCD

Simulating Cryo-EM: Cycle-Consistent Predictor–Corrector Diffusion with Biophysical Modeling

A biophysical engine turns protein structures into noise-free micrographs, and the first diffusion model for cryo-EM noise synthesis makes them look like real data.

Abstract

Learning-based cryo-EM tools need more labelled data than exists.

Single-particle cryo-electron microscopy (cryo-EM) has become a cornerstone of structural biology, enabling near-atomic resolution analysis of macromolecules through advanced computational methods. However, the development of cryo-EM processing tools is constrained by the scarcity of high-quality annotated datasets. Synthetic data generation offers a promising alternative, but existing approaches lack thorough biophysical modeling of heterogeneity and fail to reproduce the complex noise observed in real imaging.

To address these limitations, we present CryoCCD, a comprehensive simulator for cryo-EM, unifying versatile biophysical modeling with the first diffusion model for realistic noise generation. The biophysical engine provides multi-functional generation capabilities to capture authentic biological organization, and the diffusion model is enhanced with cycle consistency and predictor–corrector sampling to improve realism and structural fidelity. Extensive experiments demonstrate that CryoCCD generates structurally faithful micrographs, enhances particle picking and pose estimation, as well as achieves superior performance over state-of-the-art baselines, while also generalizing effectively to held-out protein families.

Contributions

What CryoCCD adds

01

A biophysical engine for heterogeneous specimens.

PDB structures and AlphaFold3 predictions are placed into virtual volumes with particle-type-specific distributions, multi-scale meshes and a modeled ice layer, then projected under the weak-phase approximation with a CTF.

02

The first diffusion model for cryo-EM noise.

Unpaired clean-to-real translation with cycle consistency to keep structures intact, mask-guided contrastive learning for fine detail, and a UniPC predictor–corrector sampler for noise fidelity.

03

Gains on realism and on downstream tasks.

Lowest FID and CMMD on six EMPIAR datasets, better particle picking (AUPRC) and pose estimation, and the best scores on four held-out datasets with unseen protein families or imaging conditions.

Method

From atomic structures to realistic micrographs

The pipeline has two stages. Biophysical modeling produces clean, fully annotated micrographs with known particle positions, poses and masks; realistic noise generation translates them into the real micrograph domain without paired data.

Stage 1 · Biophysical modeling

Structure library

Experimental PDB structures from ~50 kDa enzymes to >50,000 kDa viral capsids, plus AlphaFold3 models that add compositional and conformational heterogeneity.

Particle placement

RELION-derived picks and orientations blended with synthetic samples on \(S^3\); class-specific rules for enzymes, polysomes and capsids.

Multi-scale volume & ice

Scale-adaptive meshes and collision checks; a vitreous ice slab with log-normal thickness and Perlin-noise topography.

Projection & CTF

Summed electrostatic potential, weak-phase projection, and the contrast transfer function applied in Fourier space.

Potential assembly and projection

\[\Phi(\mathbf{r}) = \sum_{i=1}^{L} \rho_i\bigl(R(q_i)^{-1}(\mathbf{r}-T_i)\bigr)\]
\[P(x,y) = \int_{-z_0/2}^{z_0/2} \Phi(x,y,z)\,\mathrm{d}z\]

The electrostatic potential of all \(L\) embedded particles (density \(\rho_i\), orientation \(q_i\), translation \(T_i\)) is projected under the weak-phase approximation; the CTF is then applied in Fourier space.

Stage 2 · Realistic noise generation

Cycle consistency

A mask-conditioned generator \(G_{AB}\) and an unconditional \(G_{BA}\) are tied by an L1 cycle loss in both directions, so particle positions survive translation.

Mask-guided contrastive learning

Segmentation masks define particles as positives and background as negatives, sharpening edges, textures and spatial noise patterns.

Predictor–corrector sampling

A mask-conditioned UniPC sampler in the half log-SNR domain: a \(p\)-th order predictor and a \((p{+}1)\)-th order corrector per step.

Training objective

\[\mathcal{L} = \gamma_{\text{adv}}\mathcal{L}_{\text{adv}} + \gamma_{\text{cyc}}\mathcal{L}_{\text{cyc}} + \gamma_{\text{NCE}}\mathcal{L}_{\text{NCE}}, \qquad \gamma_{\text{adv}}=1,\ \gamma_{\text{cyc}}=3,\ \gamma_{\text{NCE}}=5\]
\[\mathcal{L}_{\text{cyc}} = \mathbb{E}_{(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{m})}\bigl\|G_{BA}(G_{AB}(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{m}))-\mathbf{x}\bigr\|_1 + \mathbb{E}_{\mathbf{y}}\bigl\|G_{AB}(G_{BA}(\mathbf{y}),\varnothing)-\mathbf{y}\bigr\|_1\]
\[\mathcal{L}_{\text{NCE}} = -\log \frac{e^{\cos(q,k^+)/\tau}}{e^{\cos(q,k^+)/\tau} + \sum_{k^-} e^{\cos(q,k^-)/\tau}}\]

The least-squares adversarial term carries the smallest weight; it serves only as an auxiliary distribution-matching regularizer on top of the diffusion sampler.

Why it matters

Turn a few real annotations into a large synthetic corpus.

Every CryoCCD micrograph comes with exact particle positions, orientations and masks, labels that are slow and error-prone to obtain from real cryo-EM data.

Because the noise is learned from real micrographs rather than assumed to be Gaussian or Poisson, models trained on the synthetic data transfer to real data. CryoCCD can help amplify limited real annotations and speed up the development of computational tools for the cryo-EM community.
